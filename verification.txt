5. SGID VERIFICATION

Requirement: files and directories created inside /shared/project
inherit the devteam group.

dev1's primary group is dev1, not devteam. Without SGID, anything dev1
creates would be group-owned by dev1.

dev1@localhost:/shared/project$ touch dev1_notes.txt
dev1@localhost:/shared/project$ echo "created by dev1" > dev1_notes.txt
dev1@localhost:/shared/project$ mkdir dev1_work
dev1@localhost:/shared/project$ ls -l
total 4
-rw-rw----. 1 dev1 devteam 16 Sep 15 01:09 dev1_notes.txt
drwxrws---. 2 dev1 devteam  6 Sep 15 01:10 dev1_work

The group owner is devteam, not dev1. SGID overrode the creating
user's primary group.

dev1@localhost:/shared/project$ ls -ld dev1_work
drwxrws---. 2 dev1 devteam 6 Sep 15 01:10 dev1_work

The new subdirectory also carries the s bit, so SGID propagates
downward and collaboration continues at deeper levels.

--- Collaboration made possible by SGID ---

dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ cat dev1_notes.txt
created by dev1
dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ echo "reviewed by dev2" >> dev1_notes.txt
dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ cat dev1_notes.txt
created by dev1
reviewed by dev2

dev2 edited a file created by dev1, through devteam group permissions.

RESULT: PASS

6. STICKY BIT VERIFICATION

Requirement: a user cannot delete another user's files, even though
the directory is group-writable.

--- dev2 cannot delete dev1's file ---

dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ rm dev1_notes.txt
rm: cannot remove 'dev1_notes.txt': Operation not permitted

--- but dev2 can delete dev2's own file ---

dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ touch dev2_notes.txt
dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ ls -l
total 4
-rw-rw----. 1 dev1 devteam 33 Sep 15 01:15 dev1_notes.txt
drwxrws---. 2 dev1 devteam  6 Sep 15 01:10 dev1_work
-rw-rw----. 1 dev2 devteam  0 Sep 15 01:16 dev2_notes.txt
dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ rm dev2_notes.txt
dev2@localhost:/shared/project$ ls -l
total 4
-rw-rw----. 1 dev1 devteam 33 Sep 15 01:15 dev1_notes.txt
drwxrws---. 2 dev1 devteam  6 Sep 15 01:10 dev1_work

This contrast is the point: the sticky bit restricts deletion to the
file's owner. It does not make the directory read-only.

--- confirmed again with a third user ---

dev3@localhost:/shared/project$ rm dev2_notes.txt
rm: cannot remove 'dev2_notes.txt': Operation not permitted

7. DEFAULT PERMISSION / UMASK VERIFICATION

Requirement: files created in the shared area are group-writable and closed to other users.

umask 007 was appended to each user's ~/.bashrc so the setting survives logout.

dev1@localhost:~$ umask
0007
dev2@localhost:~$ umask
0007

Calculation:
  files       666 - 007 = 660  ->  -rw-rw----
  directories 777 - 007 = 770  ->  drwxrwx---

Observed result:

-rw-rw----. 1 dev1 devteam 16 Sep 15 01:09 dev1_notes.txt
-rw-rw----. 1 dev2 devteam  0 Sep 15 01:16 dev2_notes.txt
-rw-rw----. 1 dev3 devteam  0 Sep 15 01:27 dev3_notes.txt
drwxrws---. 2 dev1 devteam  6 Sep 15 01:10 dev1_work

Group has rw, others have nothing, matching the requirement exactly.
The RHEL 10 default umask of 0022 would have produced -rw-r--r--,
which would have left the files readable by others and not writable
by the group.

RESULT: PASS
